## 1. Consortium and mobility [V]
DEAI replaces the long-running EMJM **BDMA**. The coordinator is ULB.
- **Sem 1:** Université Libre de Bruxelles (ULB), Belgium
- **Sem 2:** Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC), Barcelona, Spain
- European Business Intelligence Summer School (all students)
- **Sem 3:** pick one specialisation:
  - TU Wien (Austria): Knowledge-Driven Data Management and Intelligence
  - University of Padua (Italy): Business, Economics, Financial Data Science
  - Université Claude Bernard Lyon 1 (France): Data and Intelligence for Heterogeneous Systems
- **Sem 4:** master's thesis, either as an industry placement or as a research internship at any full or associated partner. Closing workshop "Business Intelligence: Innovation Meets Research".
- **Degree:** multiple national master's degrees from ULB, UPC and the specialisation university.
- Specialisation is assigned during year 1, based on preference, marks and seat availability. The ranking on the application form is for statistics only.
- Note: one search-engine summary of a UPC page listed CentraleSupélec and TU Eindhoven as partners. That looks like old BDMA-era information and conflicts with the DEAI site and manual. **[U] Ignore it.**

## 2. Duration [V]
2 years, 4 semesters, 120 ECTS, taught in English, full-time and on-site only.

## 3. Scholarship [V, except where marked]
- **Full DEAI (EMJM) scholarship:** 100% of participation costs (tuition plus full insurance), plus **EUR 1,400/month for up to 24 months** (about EUR 33,600).
- **Partial DEAI scholarship:** 100% of participation costs, with no monthly allowance. The student pays travel and living costs.
- Travel or installation allowance for full holders is **not stated** on the DEAI page. **[U]**
- Not eligible: anyone who already received an EMJM scholarship.
- Erasmus+ rule: **no more than 10% of selected applicants may share one nationality**. This affects Pakistanis, since the cap is shared with other Pakistani applicants.

## 4. Number of scholarships

## 7. Required documents [V, Manual 6.2]
Each file must be 2 MB or less, and all text documents must be in English or translated.
Mandatory uploads:
1. Passport-size photo (jpg/png)
2. **Cover letter** (PDF). This is scored on organisation, content, critical thinking, originality and factual accuracy.
3. Passport scan (PDF)
4. **CV** in English, PDF. Europass format preferred.
5. Bachelor degree certificate, certified English translation. Scholarship applicants must upload proof of the obtained degree by the dates on the website.
6. Official transcripts showing each course's credits and marks
7. **Self-evaluation Excel sheet** (macro-enabled DEAI template). Map every course to an ACM CS subject, with ACM minor subjects at no more than 10% of CS subjects. **Filling it incorrectly, or altering its formulas or protection, means automatic exclusion.**
8. Proof of English proficiency (see section 8)
9. Work-experience proof: contract or official letter in English, with dates, tasks and role. It is optional in the form but scored, so **upload it**.

Optional uploads: bachelor thesis or FYP report, other certificates, and **0-3 reference letters**. Referees upload letters themselves through the portal after receiving a 4-5 question form. Letters are optional but scored, and academic referees with an H-index are preferred, so **get 2-3**.

The university must be in the IAU WHED list or in the QS, THE or ARWU rankings, and must appear in the portal's drop-down. FAST-NUCES is very likely listed. **[U] Check the drop-down when registering.**

## 8. English requirement [V, Manual 6.1.5]
A B2 level is required. There are three accepted routes:
1. Native speaker
2. A test certificate at B2 or above, for example:
   - IELTS Academic 5.5
   - TOEFL iBT 72
   - **PTE Academic 59** (the table says "59 a 75", that is, the 59-75 B2 band)
   - Cambridge FCE
   - Oxford Test of English 111-140
   - LanguageCert B2, Linguaskill 160-179, Aptis B2, TOEIC L&R 785
   - Test results must be no more than 2 years old.
3. **Studies in English (MOI): ACCEPTED.** Upload a certificate from the university stating the programme was taught in English.
   - The manual notes that an internationally recognised certificate is *preferred* over English-medium studies. The listed selection criteria do not award points for English, so the practical effect of using MOI is **[U]**.
- **Duolingo: NOT in the accepted list.** Do not rely on it.
- **Plan:** use the FAST MOI letter. PTE Academic (59 or more) is an optional extra if you want to remove any doubt. IELTS is not needed.

## 10. Selection criteria and GPA [V; weights U]
The joint committee scores and ranks applications. The top of the ranking becomes the selected list, the next part becomes the reserve list, and the selected list goes to the EC for approval. Points are given for:
- Share of Computer Science credits in the bachelor (a BS CS should score high)
- Academic results: weighted average, converted to a 1-4 scale through the Excel sheet
- Other studies: a second degree, or extra courses counted by hours. MOOCs or certificates may help. **[U]**
- **Professional experience: points per year in an IT or BI role.** 1.5-2 years as an AI/LLM engineer counts.
- Cover letter
- Reference letters, including the referee's reputation and H-index

**No minimum GPA is stated.** The numeric weights are **not published**. **[U]**

Expected background:
- Databases and SQL, relational algebra
- Algorithms and data structures
- Probability and statistics
- UML and ER modelling
- Graph theory, set theory and logic
- Linear algebra and multivariable calculus
- Object-oriented programming

## 12. Self-funded fees [V]
- Partner country (Pakistan): **EUR 9,000/year, EUR 18,000 total**
- Programme country: EUR 4,500/year
- Fees cover academic costs, registration and insurance.
- Belgian visa for self-funded students: the guarantor must show income that can cover EUR 666/month for the student, while keeping EUR 1,254.82/month for themselves plus EUR 150/month per dependent. Visas for accompanying spouses are "very difficult, sometimes impossible".
- The EU-backed Skills & Education Guarantee loan scheme (through banks) is mentioned as an option.
