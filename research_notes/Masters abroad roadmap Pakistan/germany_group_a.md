# Germany Group A: RWTH / TU Berlin / TU Dresden / TU Darmstadt

Researched 2026-10-18 for a Pakistani applicant targeting **Winter Semester 2027/28**.
Profile: BS CS (4 yr) FAST-NUCES, CGPA 3.41/4, about 1.5-2 yrs AI/LLM engineering, **no IELTS, no GRE**; plans to use an MOI letter, with Duolingo or PTE as fallback.

Legend: **[V]** = verified on an official page this session. **[U]** = not verified this session (estimate or general knowledge; re-check before applying).

## Headline findings (read first)

1. **All three RWTH programmes require the GRE for non-EU applicants** (QR >= 75th percentile, VR >= 15th percentile, AWA >= 3.5). The score must arrive **before the 1 March deadline**. RWTH's informatics pages also say **MOI certificates are generally not accepted**. Without a GRE, RWTH is out. With a GRE taken by about Jan 2027, it is feasible. [V]
2. **TU Dresden MSc Distributed Systems Engineering has been discontinued.** The page banner reads "+++ This program will not be offered +++". [V]
3. **TU Darmstadt MSc Distributed Software Systems is closed.** The last intake was SS 2023 and the programme closed on 30 Sep 2023. Its content now sits within the TU Darmstadt MSc Computer Science. [V via official search result; programme list marks it closed]
4. **No programme in this group lists Duolingo.** PTE is accepted at TU Berlin (65 for B2+) and TU Dresden CMS (60). An MOI letter from a Pakistani university is **not** an accepted route at any of them, as far as verified. TU Dresden accepts MOI only from EU/UK/US/CA/AU/NZ/IE/HK/SG institutions. **Practical conclusion: book PTE Academic (or IELTS).** Aim for >= 65 PTE / 7.0 IELTS so that TU Darmstadt's C1 bar is also covered.
5. Tuition: none of these four states (NRW, Berlin, Saxony, Hesse) charges non-EU tuition at these universities. You pay only the semester contribution (about EUR 300-350, which includes a Deutschlandsemesterticket). [U on exact amounts]

## Grade conversion (Modified Bavarian formula, indicative)
German grade = 1 + 3 x (Nmax - Nd) / (Nmax - Nmin).
- If Nmin = 2.0: 1 + 3 x 0.59 / 2.0 = **about 1.9**
- If Nmin = 1.0: **about 1.6**

Uni-assist/anabin determine the actual value. Either result is a solid "good" grade (better than 2.5).

## 1. RWTH Aachen: MSc Data Science
- Language: English (B2 required). [V]
- Duration: 4 semesters / 120 ECTS. [U, standard]
- Fees: no tuition; semester contribution about EUR 320. [U]
- WS deadline (non-EU): application window **December to 1 March**, a hard deadline. These are the pattern dates currently published, so expect **1 Mar 2027** for WS 2027/28. [V pattern; 2027 date not yet published]
- Route: **direct**, via the RWTHonline portal. No uni-assist. [V]
- MOI accepted: **No.** The page says "Medium of instruction (MOI) certificates are in general not accepted." English proof is due at enrolment, not at application. [V]
- Duolingo / PTE: not listed on the faculty page. Third-party sources cite PTE 60 for some RWTH programmes. Duolingo appears not accepted. [U]
- GRE: **required for non-EU.** QR > 75th percentile, VR > 15th percentile, AWA >= 3.5, submitted before the deadline. [V]
- Entry: first degree in CS/Math/Physics or related, plus a fit to one of the listed profiles (e.g. Programming 8 CP, Analysis/Calculus I+II 8 CP). Module content must be comparable to RWTH's. No minimum grade is stated. Open admission (no NC). [V]
- Competitiveness at 3.41: grade is not the bottleneck. Open admission means a profile/ECTS match plus the GRE decides. **Blocked without a GRE.**
- City tech market: Aachen 2.5/5. Small city with research jobs (Fraunhofer, Ericsson, Ford R&D). Cologne/Düsseldorf are about 1 hr away.
- Source: https://sc.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/en/studium/master/master-data-science/application-for-admission/

- City tech market: Berlin 5/5. Germany's startup and AI hub, with many English-speaking jobs.

## 6. TU Dresden: MSc Computational Modeling and Simulation (CMS)
- Language: English (B2 required). [V]
- Duration: 4 semesters / 120 ECTS. [U, not on the application page]
- Fees: no tuition in Saxony for these students; semester contribution about EUR 300. [U]
- WS deadline: non-EU with a foreign degree, **1 April to 31 May** ("Application and Admission from 2026 onwards" page). Expect **31 May 2027**. The aptitude-assessment registration may close earlier. [V pattern]
- Route: **two steps.** First register in the CMS aptitude-assessment portal and keep the submission ID. Then apply via **uni-assist**, entering that ID. [V]
- MOI: accepted **only** for prior study fully in English in the EU, UK, IE, US, CA (except Quebec and New Brunswick), AU, NZ, HK or SG. **A Pakistani MOI does not qualify.** One other route exists: "a language certificate from English training during previous university studies, evaluated by the committee". FAST English courses on the transcript plus a certificate might work, but treat it as discretionary. [V]
- Duolingo: **not listed.** PTE: **accepted, minimum 60.** Others: IELTS 6.0, TOEFL iBT 80, Cambridge B2, UNIcert II. [V]
- GRE: **not required**, but "will be positively considered if submitted". [V]
- Entry: first degree in CS/Math/Natural Science/Economics/Engineering. Programming in a compiled language, calculus, linear algebra, probability, algorithms and data structures. No ECTS count or minimum grade stated. Aptitude assessment applies. [V]
- Competitiveness at 3.41: **good.** A CS graduate meets the prerequisites. The aptitude assessment weighs grade plus motivation, so a strong SOP tying LLM work to simulation/HPC helps. The fit is more HPC/scientific-computing than AI engineering.
- City tech market: Dresden 3/5. Semiconductors ("Silicon Saxony": Infineon, GlobalFoundries, Bosch, TSMC's ESMC) and research institutes. Smaller software/AI market than Berlin or Munich, and German matters more.
- Source: https://tu-dresden.de/ing/informatik/studium/studienangebot/master-studiengaenge/computational-modeling-and-simulation/application
